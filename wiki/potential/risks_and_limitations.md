# Risks and Limitations

The potential analysis for Trekie is positive, but it comes with meaningful risks. These should be treated as first-order strategic concerns, not minor cleanup items.

## Strategic Risks

### 1. Scope creep / product fragmentation
Trekie currently spans goals, habits, commitments, todos, momentum, social, and AI. That breadth is a risk if it prevents a clear wedge from emerging.

### 2. Weak commercial foundations
There is no validated business model, no acquisition strategy, and no quantitative validation yet. Strong technology alone does not create a strong business.

### 3. Premature platform ambition
A developer ecosystem and SDK play are interesting, but they can distract from the near-term consumer product that needs to prove retention first.

### 4. Competitive pressure
The productivity and habit category is crowded. Many apps fight for attention, and switching costs are low.

## Product Risks

### 1. Retention uncertainty
The hardest question is whether users form durable daily or weekly loops around Momentum, habits, and accountability.

### 2. Insight overload
If the product tries to explain too much too often, coaching can become noise instead of guidance.

### 3. Social feature drag
Social features can help retention, but they also introduce moderation, privacy, and complexity costs.

## Market Risks

### 1. Category noise
Productivity and gamification are popular categories, making differentiation harder.

### 2. Willingness-to-pay ambiguity
Users may be enthusiastic about the concept without being willing to pay premium prices.

### 3. Trend dependency
AI features are fashionable, but they must translate into durable utility, not novelty.

## Technology Risks

### 1. Local-first complexity
Offline-first architecture is powerful but operationally demanding.

### 2. AI cost and dependency
LLM-powered coaching can be expensive and model-dependent.

### 3. Testing coverage gaps
Web and API tests remain weaker than SDK coverage, which increases regression risk.

## Limitations of This Analysis

- This is a directional strategic assessment, not a validated market study.
- It does not replace user interviews, pricing tests, cohort retention analysis, or formal financial modeling.
- The strongest conclusions are about architecture and positioning; the weakest are about commercial traction, which remains unproven.

## Bottom Line

Trekie’s biggest risks are not technical. They are focus, validation, and commercialization. If those risks are addressed deliberately, the upside is significant. If they are ignored, the project risks becoming an impressive technical system without a durable market outcome.
